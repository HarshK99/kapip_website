Patentability Search
A Patentability Search is conducted to determine whether an invention meets the patentability
requirements of novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability. The search includes
analysis of prior art such as patents, scientific literature, and technical publications to identify
existing technologies that may affect the patentability and uniqueness of the invention.
Freedom to Operate
A Freedom to Operate (FTO) analysis is conducted to determine whether a product, process,
or service to be developed, manufactured, used, or commercialized without infringing the
valid patent rights of third parties. The FTO search involves identifying relevant patents and
patent applications in the target jurisdictions, followed by a detailed analysis of patent claims
to evaluate potential infringement risks.

State-of-the-Art study
A State-of-the-Art study involves mapping the current technological landscape, tracking
emerging trends, and recognizing key players and existing solutions within the field. We
conduct a broad search to understand technological developments in a specific field which
involves a comprehensive review of existing knowledge, innovations, and advancements
across patents, scientific literature, technical publications, and industrial practices. This
process helps in understanding the current state of technology, highlighting advancements,
and recognizing developments across the domain.

Patent Landscape Analysis: Comprehensive study of existing patents within a specific
technology domain.

Invalidity and validity searches
We conduct a comprehensive prior art analysis to evaluate the strength and enforceability of a
patent. A validity search determines whether a granted patent complies with the requirements
of novelty, inventive step, and patentability in view of existing disclosures, while in an
invalidity search identifies prior art that may challenge or potentially invalidate the patent
claims. These assessments involve a detailed review of patents, patent applications, scientific
literature, technical documents, and other publicly available information. They are widely
used in litigation, opposition proceedings, licensing negotiations, and strategic intellectual
property management to enable informed legal and commercial decision-making.

Patent Drafting

Provisional Specification
A Provisional Specification is drafted to secure an early priority date for an invention before
filing the complete patent specification. Provisional specification drafting helps inventors
establish ownership of the invention while allowing additional time for further research,
development, and refinement prior to filing the complete specification.
Complete Specification
A Complete Specification is a detailed patent document that fully describes the invention,
includes claims defining the scope of protection, drawings (if required), and an abstract of the
invention. The Complete Specification should be filed within 12 months from the date of
filing the provisional application.
